The Strategy in One Line

Why the free in-home water test is the right tip of the spear

People don't wake up wanting to buy a $2,200 water softener. They wake up annoyed about dry skin, orange stains, chlorine taste, or a rotten-egg smell — and worried something might be in their water. A free, no-obligation in-home water test is the perfect bridge: it's a low-friction "yes," it's genuinely valuable, and it puts a Williamson Water expert at the kitchen sink with a quote in hand.

Low-friction yes

"Free 30-minute test" converts far better than "get a quote." It removes price anxiety and commitment at the top of the funnel.

High intent, qualified

Anyone who books a test into their home has a real water concern and is a homeowner in your service area — the only two things that matter.

Sells itself on-site

Hardness, TDS, chlorine, iron, and pH results on the kitchen counter make the problem undeniable — and the solution obvious.

The whole engine in one sentence: Run paid + organic traffic → a single-purpose landing page → instant automated follow-up books a test → reminder automation gets them to show → Ken (or a tech) tests, educates, and quotes → nurture automation closes the rest.

Where the Leads Come From

2Lead sources to run traffic to

Start with the two channels that produce booked tests fastest, then layer the rest. Everything points at the same landing page so tracking and follow-up stay clean.

Tier 1 — Launch first

  • Meta (Facebook/Instagram) lead ads — "Is your Western Slope water hard? Free in-home test." Hyper-local, homeowner-targeted, image of stained fixtures vs. clean glass.
  • Google Search ads — high-intent keywords: "water test near me," "hard water Montrose," "well water testing Delta CO," "rotten egg smell water."
  • Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — pay-per-lead, "Google Guaranteed" badge, top of local results. Best ROI for trades.

Tier 2 — Scale & compound

  • Retargeting — anyone who hit the landing page but didn't book, on Meta + Google display.
  • Your own site — add a "Free Water Test" CTA banner to williamsonwater.co (you already have /schedule + /quote to plug in).
  • Nextdoor + local Facebook groups — neighborhood-level trust; great for Montrose, Olathe, Cedaredge.
  • Database reactivation — past quotes & no-decisions get re-offered the free test (cheapest leads you'll ever get).
  • Referral + review engine — every install triggers a referral ask and a review request.
One tracked number, one page. Use a tracking phone number (CallRail) and a single landing page so every dollar is attributable. If you can't measure cost-per-booked-test by channel, you can't scale it.
The Conversion Asset

3The landing page leads run to

A dedicated, single-goal page — williamsonwater.co/free-water-test — with one job: book the test. No top nav, no distractions, one offer. (Your Next.js site can host this as a new route alongside the existing pages.)

Above the fold

  • Headline: "Find out exactly what's in your water — free, in your home."
  • Sub: 30 minutes · no obligation · same-week appointments
  • One CTA button → embedded calendar / short form (name, phone, ZIP, biggest concern)
  • Trust strip: WQA Certified · CO Master Plumber #ML-204881 · 4.9★ local reviews · Serving the Western Slope since 2009

Below the fold

  • What we test: hardness (gpg), TDS, chlorine/chloramine, iron, pH, plus a bacteria screen for wells
  • What to expect: 3-step "we test → we explain → you decide" — zero pressure
  • Social proof: 2–3 local reviews with town names (Montrose, Ridgway, Paonia)
  • Risk reversal: "No sales pressure. No cost. Keep the results either way."
  • Scarcity: "Limited test slots each week within 75 miles of Montrose"
Qualify on the form, not on the phone. Two questions — "Do you own your home?" and "City or well water?" — plus ZIP let automation auto-route renters/out-of-area leads to a soft no and book the rest instantly.
